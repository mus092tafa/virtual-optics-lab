About the software

Nothing on the screen is drawn by hand.

Students build an experiment on a virtual bench from a laser or a lamp, lenses, mirrors, slits, gratings, polarisers and a screen. The lab works out what reaches the screen from where each part stands. Move the screen out of the image plane and the image blurs. Narrow a slit and the diffraction pattern widens.

Calculated from the bench

Positions, sizes, blur, brightness, colour and fringe spacing all come from the physics, never from a drawing.

1/f = 1/do + 1/di

The right model for the setup

The solver uses geometric rays, Gaussian beams or wave optics, depending on what is on the rail. When a setup is outside what a model covers, the lab says so.

w(z) = w₀√(1 + (z/zR)²)

A notebook that checks the work

Students record their readings, the notebook fits the straight line, and the result is compared with theory, uncertainty included.

λ = Δy · d / L

Two editions

One for the teacher, one for the class.

Both editions contain the whole laboratory. They differ in what they do with exams, so a student’s laptop never holds the answer key.

Teacher edition

Write the exam, run the room, mark the answers.

  • The whole laboratory, all 22 experiments.
  • Write exams with written answers, multiple choice, or measurements on the virtual bench with the calculated values withheld.
  • Open a classroom over the local Wi-Fi with no internet. The students’ laptops find it, and the clock runs from yours.
  • Mark the answers, export the results as PDF and CSV, and keep every sitting in the records on your laptop.
Download the teacher edition
Student edition

Practise on the bench, then sit the exam.

  • The whole laboratory, all 22 experiments, with guides and the notebook.
  • Join the classroom: find the teacher’s laptop on the network and sit the exam.
  • Sit an exam from a file on the same computer, without the network.
  • No exam-writing or marking code is built into it, and the answer key never reaches it.

Experiments

22 experiments in six topics.

Each experiment comes with a guide, the equations it rests on, a notebook for the readings and a demonstration that runs it for you. The numbers are the ones the app uses.

Rays & Surfaces

4
  1. 1
    Law of Reflection

    Tilt the mirror and read both angles from the protractor.

    θr = θi
  2. 2
    Refraction: Snell’s Law

    Find the index of the second medium from how far the ray bends.

    n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
  3. 14
    Brewster’s Angle

    Find the angle where reflected p-polarised light vanishes.

    tan θB = n₂ / n₁
  4. 15
    Prism: Minimum Deviation

    Measure the index of the glass and watch it change with wavelength.

    n = sin((A + δmin)/2) / sin(A/2)

Lenses & Mirrors

5
  1. 3
    Convex Lens: Focal Length

    Find f from the object and image distances.

    1/f = 1/do + 1/di
  2. 4
    Lens Magnification

    See how image size and orientation follow the object position.

    m = −di / do
  3. 8
    Tungsten Light Source

    A broadband, extended source in an optical system.

    black body, T = 2900 K
  4. 10
    Concave Lens: Focal Length

    Measure a lens whose image cannot be caught on a screen.

    f < 0, virtual image
  5. 16
    Concave Mirror: Focal Length

    Find the focal length and radius of curvature of a mirror.

    f = R / 2

  1. 5

    Follow how a Gaussian beam spreads and how a lens focuses it.

Diffraction & Interference

5
  1. 6
    Single-Slit Diffraction

    A narrower slit gives a wider pattern.

    a sin θ = mλ
  2. 7
    Double-Slit Interference

    Measure the laser’s wavelength from the fringe spacing.

    Δy = λL / d
  3. 11
    Diffraction Grating

    Measure the wavelength from the positions of the orders.

    d sin θm = mλ
  4. 12
    Circular Aperture: Airy Pattern

    Find the aperture’s diameter from its first dark ring.

    sin θ = 1.22 λ / D
  5. 9
    Michelson Interferometer

    Count the fringes that pass while one mirror moves.

    λ = 2Δd / N

Polarisation

1
  1. 13
    Malus’s Law

    Turn the analyser and measure the light it lets through.

    I = I₀ cos²θ

The Eye

6
  1. 17
    Accommodation and the Near Point

    The lens changes shape to focus near and far.

    near point at 1/(A − K)
  2. 18
    Myopia, Hyperopia and Glasses

    Find the glasses that move the focus onto the retina.

    Fs = K / (1 + dK)
  3. 19
    Ageing and Reading Glasses

    The near point recedes as the lens stiffens with age.

    A = 18.5 − 0.30 × age (D)
  4. 20
    Astigmatism and the Cylinder Lens

    Two corneal curvatures, two focal lines, one cylinder to correct them.

    P = (n′ − 1) / r per meridian
  5. 21
    Chromatic Aberration

    The eye focuses blue light in front of red.

    0.85 D between 486 and 656 nm
  6. 22
    Spherical Aberration and the Pupil

    Rays through the edge of the pupil focus in front of those near the axis.

    n sin i = n′ sin i′, traced exactly
